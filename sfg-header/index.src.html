About SFG

A Palestinian‑led mentorship initiative

Scholarships for Ghazza (SFG) is a Palestinian‑led mentorship programme that helps students from Gaza apply for higher education scholarships. With Israel destroying all of Gaza’s universities, students are left with no place to continue their education. Yet for generations, Palestinians have valued education as a path to hope and opportunity. That’s why SFG exists: to guide students through the application process and help them secure scholarships to study abroad.

What we do

  • Mentor

    Every student is paired with an academic mentor and an SFG organiser, bringing hope and purpose back to studies the genocide brought to a halt.

  • Guide

    Mentors help students curate applications and write strong personal statements, CVs and recommendation letters, then prepare them with mock interviews and English practice for TOEFL, IELTS and Duolingo.

  • Connect

    We guide students to existing scholarships and universities abroad, help them choose courses and request fee waivers, and support them as they write to universities and scholarship boards.

What we don’t do

  • Charge students

    SFG is free, always. We even offer small support towards application fees, visa costs, English tests, and internet and electricity access.

  • Write applications for students

    Mentors don’t complete applications. They give students the skills and confidence to do it themselves.

  • Award or promise scholarships

    We don’t provide scholarships. We guide students to existing ones and help them tell their story in a way that fits each scholarship’s goals.

How it works

Five steps, one mentor the whole way

Free from the first form to the final decision, with a mentor who gives you the skills and confidence to do it yourself.

  1. Apply to SFG

    Tell us what you study, where you hope to go and the degree you are aiming for. SFG is free, always.

  2. Meet your mentor

    You join a WhatsApp group with your academic mentor and an SFG organiser, where you share documents, get feedback and stay on track.

  3. Choose your scholarships

    Together you find the existing scholarships worth applying for, check every eligibility requirement and choose your courses.

  4. Write, draft by draft

    Your CV, personal statement and scholarship essays, refined with your mentor until they tell your story the way each scholarship is looking for.

  5. Interview and submit

    Mock interviews and English practice prepare you for the real thing, and your mentor helps you communicate with universities and scholarship boards until the decision arrives.
