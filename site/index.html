About SFG

A Palestinian-led mentorship initiative

Scholarships for Ghazza (SFG) is a Palestinian-led mentorship programme that helps students from Gaza apply for higher education scholarships. With Israel destroying all of Gaza’s universities, students are left with no place to continue their education. Yet for generations, Palestinians have valued education as a path to hope and opportunity. That’s why SFG exists: to guide students through the application process and help them secure scholarships to study abroad.

What we do

  • Mentor

    Every student is paired with an academic mentor and an SFG organiser, bringing hope and purpose back to studies the genocide brought to a halt.

  • Guide

    Mentors help students curate applications and write strong personal statements, CVs and recommendation letters, then prepare them with mock interviews and English practice for TOEFL, IELTS and Duolingo.

  • Connect

    We guide students to existing scholarships and universities abroad, help them choose courses and request fee waivers, and support them as they write to universities and scholarship boards.

What we don’t do

  • Charge students

    SFG is free, always. We even offer small support towards application fees, visa costs, English tests, and internet and electricity access.

  • Write applications for students

    Mentors don’t complete applications. They give students the skills and confidence to do it themselves.

  • Award or promise scholarships

    We don’t provide scholarships. We guide students to existing ones and help them tell their story in a way that fits each scholarship’s goals.

How it works

One mentor beside you, every step of the way

Five free steps, from the first form to the final decision. Your mentor builds your skills and confidence along the way.

  1. Apply to SFG

    Tell us what you study, where you hope to go and the degree you are aiming for. SFG is free, always.

    Start your application (opens the application form in a new tab)

  2. Meet your mentor

    You join a WhatsApp group with your academic mentor and an SFG organiser, where you share documents, get feedback and stay on track.

  3. Choose your scholarships

    Together you find the existing scholarships worth applying for, check every eligibility requirement and choose your courses.

  4. Write, draft by draft

    Your CV, personal statement and scholarship essays, refined with your mentor until they tell your story the way each scholarship is looking for.

  5. Interview and submit

    Mock interviews and English practice prepare you for the real thing, and your mentor helps you communicate with universities and scholarship boards until the decision arrives.

In Remembrance

In the spirit of the SfG community and the mission that unites us, we always remember with deep sorrow our mentees Sha’ban A-Dalo, Dalia Obaid, her twin sister Sally Obaid, and Nada Issa, who were martyred during the Israeli genocide on Gaza. Sha’ban, Dalia, Sally, and Nada were brilliant young students striving to obtain scholarships to pursue their education abroad in a stable and supportive environment after the brutal genocide on Gaza destroyed their universities and placed them under unimaginable circumstances. Despite all the horrors they endured, they never gave up. They insisted on achieving their dreams and securing their right to a high-quality education — a right each and every human being deserves.

However, the Zionist forces did not allow that dream to continue. As with so many stories of Gaza’s martyrs, people with normal lives, loving families and friends, and great ambitions, their stories ended in tragedy. The occupation forces attacked Sha’ban’s family tent near Al-Aqsa Hospital in Deir al-Balah and burned him alive along with several of his family members. They also killed Dalia and her twin sister Sally, who had both been accepted into a PhD program at the University of Waterloo in Canada, while they were in the process of applying for study permits and preparing to cross the Rafah border into Egypt. Nada was also martyred during the genocide while preparing to pursue graduate studies in business and management. A talented translator and project coordinator, she combined language, strategy, and leadership in her work and dreamed of continuing her education through an MBA to contribute to her community’s development.

Today, we dedicate this page to the memory of Sha’ban, Dalia, Sally, and Nada, four of our brightest mentees whose dreams and determination continue to inspire us. Through this work, and through every effort to help Gazan students access education and rebuild their futures, we honour their lives, their ambitions, and their unshakable belief in hope and learning. We pray that Allah (SWT) has mercy upon them and grants them Jannat al-Firdaws. Ameen.

Ahmed IssaInitiative Founder
